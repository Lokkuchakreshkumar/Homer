Washington, D.C.

Washington, D.C. is the capital city of the United States, named for George Washington and founded in 1790 along the Potomac River. It is a compact city of monuments, museums, and federal offices, and it is not part of any state.

The White House is the official residence and principal workplace of the president of the United States. It has been the home of every president since John Adams took up residence in 1800.

The building sits at 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue NW, a short walk from Lafayette Square. Its north front faces the avenue while its south front looks out over the Ellipse, and the address has become shorthand for the presidency itself.

Originally known as the Executive Mansion, the residence was designed by James Hoban, an Irish-born architect who won the competition for the commission in 1792. It was rebuilt after British forces set it alight in 1814.

The National Mall stretches west from the Capitol to the Lincoln Memorial, lined by the Smithsonian museums. The Washington Monument, an obelisk of marble, stands at its centre and remains the tallest structure in the city.

Getting around

The Washington Metro opened in 1976 and its vaulted concrete stations are recognisable by their coffered ceilings. Fares are distance-based and paid with a SmarTrip card at the faregate before boarding.

Traffic in the district is notoriously heavy on weekday mornings, and much of the street parking near federal buildings is restricted to permit holders during business hours.
